How the numbers are worked out

  • A premium counts as paid once its due date is today or earlier.
  • Payments fall on the same day of the month as the start date, or the last day of a shorter month.
  • Yearly increases apply from each policy anniversary.
  • Policies with no maturity date, such as health cover, are projected to the year set in Settings.
  • Implied return compares all premiums with the maturity benefit you enter. It ignores the value of cover and tax benefits.
  • Health details follow IRDAI, GST and income tax rules checked in September 2026. Open Health rules on a health policy for a summary.
  • Your data stays in this browser only. Use Backup to keep a copy.

Health insurance rules in India

Checked in September 2026 against IRDAI, GST Council and income tax sources. Rules change, and your policy wording always comes first.

Waiting periods

Pre-existing conditions can have a waiting period of at most 36 months. Many policies also have a 30-day initial wait for illnesses, while accidents are covered from day one. Waiting periods carry over when you port to another insurer with no break in cover.

Five-year moratorium

After 60 months of continuous cover, an insurer cannot reject a claim for non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except for proven fraud. A lapse can reset it, so port to a new insurer instead of buying a fresh policy.

Grace period

30 days after the due date for yearly, half-yearly and quarterly premiums, and 15 days for monthly instalments. Pay within it and your benefits continue.

Switching insurer

IRDAI's 2024 master circular lets you apply to port between 60 and 30 days before renewal. The new insurer must credit the waiting periods you have already served.

Senior citizens

Since January 2025, insurers cannot raise premiums for people aged 60 and over by more than 10% a year without IRDAI's approval.

GST

No GST on individual health policies, including family floater and senior citizen plans, for premiums paid on or after 22 September 2025. Group and employer policies still carry 18%.

Tax (Section 80D)

Old tax regime only. Each year you can deduct up to ₹25,000 for yourself, your spouse and children (₹50,000 if any of them is 60 or over), plus up to ₹25,000 for your parents (₹50,000 if a parent is 60 or over). A preventive health check-up of up to ₹5,000 counts inside these limits. Premiums must be paid by a non-cash mode. From tax year 2026-27 this section is numbered 126, with the same limits.

Sync with another device

Send your ledger from this device to your phone or PC. Open this same web address on both devices, then choose Send on one and Receive on the other. The data goes directly between the two devices and is encrypted on the way. A free public service (PeerJS) only helps the devices find each other. Both devices need internet.
